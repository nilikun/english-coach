**Hi Klaus,**
✅ Fine if Klaus already signs his emails "Klaus". If you've only seen his full name so far, *Dear Mr. [surname],* is safer for a first email to a new German partner.

**①** Noted with thanks on your proposal.
**→** Thank you for your proposal.
- 🗣️ **Singapore office phrase** `noted-thanks`: To an overseas reader, *Noted with thanks* sounds stiff and telegraphic, and *noted … on* isn't standard grammar. Just thank him for the specific thing.

**②** Our team got discuss already and overall can, but the timeline a bit tight leh.
**→** Our team has discussed it, and overall it works for us, but the timeline is a bit tight.
- ❌ **Completed action** `sg-got` `sg-already`: *got discuss already* is how Singlish marks a finished action. In standard English that's the present perfect, *has discussed*, and *discuss* needs an object (*discussed it*).
- 🗣️ **"can" as a full answer** `sg-can`: Klaus won't know what *overall can* refers to. Say what is acceptable: *overall it works for us*.
- ❌ **Missing "is"** `sg-copula`: *the timeline **is** a bit tight*.
- 🗣️ **Particle** `sg-particle`: in speech, *leh* softens a complaint. In writing to someone overseas it just looks like a typo. *A bit* already does the softening, so you can drop *leh*.

**③** Last time we do similar project also take 3 months.
**→** A similar project we did previously took 3 months.
- ❌ **Past tense** `past-tense`: this happened in the past, so *did* and *took*, not *do* and *take*.
- ❌ **Article** `article`: a singular countable noun needs *a* or *the*: ***a** similar project*.
- ⚠️ **"Last time"** `sg-last-time`: in Singapore usage this means "previously". To many international readers *last time* means "the most recent occasion" and sounds a bit odd here. *Previously* is clear to everyone.
- ⚠️ **"also"** `sg-also`: in standard English, *also* means "in addition", so *also took 3 months* makes the reader ask "also, compared to what?" If you mean "this one will need about the same time", say it: *…took 3 months, and we expect this one to need about the same.* Only add that if it's true. Otherwise drop *also*.

**④** Pls revert by this Fri if can extend to end Q2.
**→** Could you let us know by Friday, 23 October, whether the deadline can be extended to the end of Q2?
- 🗣️ **"revert"** `revert`: this is the most important fix. In UK and US English, *revert* means "go back to an earlier state", so Klaus may read *Pls revert* as "please undo it". Use *let us know*, *reply* or *get back to us*.
- ❌ **"if" vs "whether"** `if-whether`: *Please reply by Friday if you can extend* can be read as "reply only if the answer is yes". *Let us know whether…* asks for an answer either way.
- ❌ **Missing subject and object** `sg-can-check`: *if can extend* doesn't say who extends what. Name it: *whether **the deadline** can be extended*.
- 🗣️ **Too blunt** `too-blunt`: a bare imperative (*Pls revert*) reads as an order to a European reader. One softener (*Could you…*) is enough, and sentence ③ already gives your reason.
- 💡 **Exact dates** `exact-date`: *Pls* and *Fri* are chat abbreviations, so write them in full. Better still, give the date, since he's six or seven hours behind you. Also check what *end of Q2* means to him: his company may use a different financial year. If you mean 30 June, write *the end of June*.

**⑤** Also PFA the soft copy of our company profile.
**→** I've also attached our company profile.
- 🗣️ **Abbreviation** `pfa`: *PFA* (please find attached) isn't widely known outside South and Southeast Asia. *I've attached…* is shorter and clear to everyone.
- 🗣️ **"soft copy"** `soft-copy`: it's an email attachment, so it can only be a soft copy. Leave it out.

**Thanks & regards,**
✅ Acceptable. *Best regards,* is a slightly safer default for a first email to someone abroad.

2. **Singapore office phrases** (①④⑤): *Noted with thanks*, *revert*, *PFA*, *soft copy*. They're normal in Singapore offices and unclear or stiff to overseas readers.
