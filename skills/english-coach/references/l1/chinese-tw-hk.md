# Taiwan and Hong Kong writers

Load this together with `chinese.md`. The core Chinese habits apply; this file adds what is specific to Taiwan and Hong Kong, and how to write explanations for these readers.

## Explanation language

Write in 繁體中文 and use the user's regional vocabulary. A Mainland word in a Taiwan reader's explanation feels foreign, the same way British spelling feels foreign in a US memo.

| Concept | Taiwan | Hong Kong | Mainland (avoid for these users) |
|---|---|---|---|
| information | 資訊 | 資訊 | 信息 |
| software | 軟體 | 軟件 | 软件 |
| network / internet | 網路 | 網絡 | 网络 |
| quality | 品質 | 質素 / 品質 | 质量 |
| video | 影片 | 影片 / 視頻 | 视频 |
| project | 專案 | 項目 | 项目 |
| email | 電子郵件 / email | 電郵 | 邮件 |
| print | 列印 | 打印 | 打印 |
| default | 預設 | 預設 | 默认 |

If you can't tell whether the user is in Taiwan or Hong Kong, use Taiwan forms and neutral wording, and switch as soon as the user shows a preference. Cantonese particles (嘅、咗、喺、俾、啲) mean Hong Kong.

## Spelling

- **Hong Kong** generally follows British spelling (*colour, organise, centre*). Don't "correct" it to American spelling. Check it's consistent.
- **Taiwan** usually follows American spelling. Same rule: consistency over preference.

## Taiwan: common patterns

| slug | Source | Common mistake | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wish-you` | 祝你有美好的一天 | *Wish you have a nice day.* | *Have a nice day.* / *Hope you have a good day.* |
| `xinku` | 辛苦了 | *You are so hard-working.* / *You worked hard.* | *Thanks for all your work on this.* |
| `mafan-ni` | 麻煩你 | *Trouble you to…* / *Please help me to…* | *Could you…?* |
| `buhaoyisi` | 不好意思 | *Sorry* at the start of every request | Use *sorry* only for a real mistake or delay. *Quick question:* or *Could you…* is enough. |
| `qingwen` | 請問 | *May I ask…* before every question | Ask the question directly; *Could you tell me…* if you need softening. |
| `office-case` | 這個 case | *this case* for a project or client | *this project*, *this account*, *this deal* (see `mixed-input.md`). |
| `office-follow` | 我來 follow | *I will follow this.* | *I'll follow up on this.* / *I'll take care of this.* |
| `jingzhu` | 敬祝 商祺 / 順頌 時祺 | literal translation of the formal closing | *Best regards,* / *Kind regards,* |
| `do-you-want` | 你要不要…？ | *Do you want to join the meeting?* to a senior | *Would you like to join…?* / *Would you be available to join…?* |
| `very-appreciate` | 非常感謝 | *Very appreciate your help.* | *I really appreciate your help.* |

## Hong Kong: common patterns

Hong Kong business English is shaped by Cantonese and by older British office conventions. Many of these phrases are normal inside Hong Kong; flag them when the reader is international, and explain that context.

| slug | Source | Common mistake | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| `send-you-later` | 我 send 俾你 | *I will send you later.* | *I'll send it to you later.* (*send* needs the thing being sent) |
| `revert` | 回覆 | *Please revert at your earliest convenience.* | *Please reply / get back to me by Friday.* (*revert* = go back to an earlier state for most international readers) |
| `be-reminded` | 請注意 | *Please be reminded that…* | *A reminder that…* / *Please note that…* |
| `noted-thanks` | 收到，多謝 | *Noted with thanks.* | *Thanks, noted.* / *Thanks, I'll take care of it.* (fine internally in HK) |
| `attached-ref` | 附件供參考 | *Please find the attached file for your reference.* | *I've attached the file.* / *The file is attached.* |
| `kindly-advise` | 請告知 | *Kindly advise.* | *Could you let me know…?* and say what exactly. |
| `ot` | OT | *I need to OT tonight.* | *I need to work late tonight.* / *work overtime*. |
| `chop` | 蓋印 | *Please chop the form.* | *Please stamp the form.* (*chop* is HK and SG usage) |
| `fill-form` | 填表 | *Please fill the form.* | *Please fill in the form* (BrE) / *fill out* (AmE). |
| `staffs` | 員工 | *All staffs are invited.* | *All staff are invited.* (*staff* is a group noun) |
| `discuss-about` | 討論一下 | *Let's discuss about it.* | *Let's discuss it.* |
| `add-oil` | 加油 | *Add oil!* | Fine with Hong Kong colleagues (it's even in the OED). For others: *Good luck!* / *You've got this.* |

## Academic writing

Hong Kong and Taiwan universities publish heavily in English, so the core Brittman habits in `chinese.md` apply in full. Taiwan writers in particular show the `article`, `long-sentence` and `front-loaded-purpose` habits. Hong Kong writers often have fewer grammar errors and more register issues: legal-style stiffness, and British office phrases carried into papers.
