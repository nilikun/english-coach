# Singapore writers

Load this for Singaporean writers, and with `chinese.md` for Singaporeans whose home language is Mandarin or another Chinese language. Much of it also helps Malaysian writers.

## Context to respect

Singapore is an English-medium country. Most writers here are fluent, and many write English better than Mandarin. The issues are rarely basic grammar. They are:

1. **Singlish** (Colloquial Singapore English) slipping into formal writing. Singlish is a real, rule-governed variety with its own grammar. It's great on WhatsApp and wrong in a client report, the same way American slang is wrong in a US annual report.
2. **Singapore business English**: old British office phrases that are normal in Singapore and sound stiff or unclear abroad.
3. **Mandarin transfer** for writers who think in Chinese first (see `chinese.md`).

Never call Singlish "broken English". Say "this works with Singapore colleagues; for an overseas or formal reader, write…"

**Explanation language**: English by default. Switch to 简体中文 if the user writes to you in Chinese.
**Spelling**: British (*colour, organisation, programme*). Don't Americanize it.

## Singlish features to flag in formal writing

| slug | Feature | Example | Formal |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sg-particle` | Discourse particles: *lah, leh, lor, meh, hor, sia, ah* | *Can lah, no problem.* | *Yes, that works.* |
| `sg-can` | *Can* as a full answer or question | *Can or not?* / *Can.* | *Would that be possible?* / *Yes, that's fine.* |
| `sg-already` | *Already* marking completion | *I send already.* | *I've sent it.* |
| `sg-got` | *Got* for "there is / have" | *Got problem with the file.* | *There's a problem with the file.* |
| `sg-is-it` | *Is it* as a tag question | *You're free tomorrow, is it?* | *Are you free tomorrow?* |
| `sg-one` | Sentence-final *one* | *He very particular one.* | *He's very particular.* |
| `sg-copula` | Missing *is/are* | *The client very happy.* | *The client is very happy.* |
| `sg-last-time` | *Last time* = "in the past" | *Last time we used vendor A.* | *We used to use vendor A.* / *Previously, we…* |
| `sg-same-same` | Reduplication | *same same*, *small small* | *the same*, *quite small* |
| `sg-send` | *Send* = accompany | *I'll send you to the airport.* | *I'll take you to the airport.* |
| `sg-follow` | *Follow* = go with | *Can I follow you to the meeting?* | *Can I join you at the meeting?* |
| `sg-borrow` | *Borrow* for *lend* | *Can you borrow me your charger?* | *Can you lend me your charger?* |
| `sg-take` | *Take* = eat or drink | *I don't take beef.* | Fine in speech; in writing *I don't eat beef.* |
| `sg-slang` | Local words from Malay, Hokkien, etc.: *shiok, paiseh, sian, chope, kiasu, alamak, jialat, steady* | *Paiseh for the late reply.* | *Sorry for the late reply.* |

## Singapore business English

These are standard in many Singapore offices. Flag them for overseas readers, new external contacts, and formal documents, and leave them alone when the user says the email stays internal.

| slug | Phrase | Overseas readers may hear | Write instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| `revert` | *Please revert by Friday.* | "change back by Friday" | *Please reply by Friday.* |
| `noted-thanks` | *Noted with thanks.* | stiff, telegraphic | *Thanks, noted.* / *Thank you, I'll take care of it.* |
| `be-informed` | *Please be informed that…* / *Kindly be advised…* | bureaucratic | State the information, or *Please note that…* |
| `pfa` | *PFA* (please find attached) | unclear abbreviation | *I've attached…* |
| `do-the-needful` | *Kindly do the needful.* | vague; reader doesn't know what to do | Say what you need: *Could you approve the invoice by Friday?* |
| `sg-mc` | *I'm on MC today.* | "MC" unknown abroad | *I'm on medical leave today.* / *I'm off sick today.* |
| `sg-off-day` | *Tomorrow is my off day.* | understood, but informal | *I'm off tomorrow.* / *I'm on leave tomorrow.* |
| `sg-outstation` | *I'm outstation next week.* | not standard outside South/Southeast Asia | *I'm travelling for work next week.* |
| `soft-copy` | *Send me the soft copy.* | understood, a bit dated | *Send me the file / the PDF.* |
| `sg-chop` | *Please chop the document.* | confusing | *Please stamp / sign the document.* |
| `sg-can-check` | *Can help me check?* | missing subject | *Could you check this for me?* |
| `sg-handphone` | *handphone*, *HP number* | unclear | *mobile number* / *phone number* |
| `sg-the-same` | *Kindly confirm the same.* | unclear what "the same" refers to | *Could you confirm the delivery date?* |

## Tone

Singapore email often has the opposite problem from Japanese email: **too blunt** in writing, because short spoken Singlish forms are friendly face to face but curt on screen. *Pls send by today.* reads as a command to a European or American reader. Suggest adding one softener and a reason: *Could you send it by 5 pm today? We need it for the client call tomorrow.*
