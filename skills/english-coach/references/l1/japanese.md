# Japanese-speaking writers: habits to look for

Explain in 日本語 unless the user writes to you in English. Keep explanations concrete. Japanese learners are often taught many rules and few reasons, so say *why* in one line ("英語の読み手は最初の一文で用件を知りたいから").

## 1. Grammar Japanese doesn't mark

| slug | Habit | Example → fix | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `article` | Articles missing or wrong. The most common problem, as for Chinese writers. | *I attended meeting yesterday.* → *I attended **the** meeting yesterday.* | Japanese has no articles. Singular countable nouns need *a/the/my/this*. |
| `plural` | Singular/plural not marked or wrong. | *We have three problem.* → *problems*; *many informations* → *a lot of information*. | Japanese nouns don't mark number. |
| `missing-subject` | Subject left out. | *Will send the file tomorrow.* → *I will send the file tomorrow.* | Japanese drops the subject when it's understood. English needs it, except in very informal notes. |
| `missing-object` | Object left out after a transitive verb. | *I checked.* (what?) → *I checked the file.*; *Please confirm.* → *Please confirm the date.* | Same as above. |
| `tense` | Present tense for finished actions; tense shifts mid-paragraph. | *Yesterday I send the report.* → *sent*. | Japanese past (〜た) and perfect don't map neatly to English. |
| `until-by` | *Until* for a deadline (までに). | *Please submit until Friday.* → *by Friday*. | *Until* = continuing up to a time; *by* = no later than. |
| `looking-forward-to` | *Look forward to* + base verb. | *I look forward to hear from you.* → *hearing*. | *To* here is a preposition. |
| `almost` | *Almost* + noun (ほとんどの). | *Almost Japanese companies…* → *Most Japanese companies…* / *Almost all…* | *Almost* modifies *all*, not the noun. |
| `there-is-many` | Number agreement with *there is*. | *There is many reasons.* → *There are*. | |
| `consider-about` | Extra preposition after a transitive verb. | *consider about*, *discuss about*, *contact with* → *consider*, *discuss*, *contact*. | |
| `relative-clause` | Long modifier before the noun, translated in the same order. | *The by our team developed system* → *The system our team developed*. | English puts long modifiers after the noun. |

## 2. Word choice and wasei-eigo

Japanese uses many English-looking words with different meanings. These are the ones that cause real misunderstandings. For more office katakana (フィックス, リスケ, ブラッシュアップ…), read only the *Japanese office katakana* table in `mixed-input.md`.

| slug | 和製・カタカナ | Mistake | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wasei-claim` | クレーム | *We received a claim from the customer.* | *a complaint* (*claim* = 主張・保険請求) |
| `wasei-smart` | スマート | *She is smart.* (meaning slim) | *slim*; *smart* = 頭がいい |
| `wasei-naive` | ナイーブ | *He is naive.* (meaning sensitive) | *sensitive*; *naive* = 世間知らず (negative) |
| `wasei-tension` | テンション | *My tension is high.* | *I'm excited / in a good mood.* (*tension* = 緊張・対立) |
| `wasei-challenge` | チャレンジする | *I challenged the exam.* | *I took on / attempted the exam.* (*challenge* = 異議を唱える) |
| `wasei-mypace` | マイペース | *He works at my pace.* | *He works at his own pace.* |
| `wasei-image-up` | イメージアップ | *image up* | *improve our image* |
| `wasei-after-service` | アフターサービス | *after service* | *after-sales service*, *customer support* |
| `wasei-consent` | コンセント | *consent* (meaning outlet) | *outlet* (US), *socket* (UK) |
| `wasei-cunning` | カンニング | *cunning* (meaning cheating) | *cheating* |
| `wasei-note-pc` | ノートパソコン | *note PC* | *laptop* |
| `wasei-skill-up` | スキルアップ | *skill up* | *improve my skills*, *upskill* |
| `wasei-merit` | メリット・デメリット | *merit and demerit* | *advantages and disadvantages*, *pros and cons* |
| `wasei-trouble` | トラブル | *We had a trouble.* | *We had a problem / an issue.* (*trouble* is uncountable) |
| `wasei-evidence` | エビデンス | *We need the evidence.* (for records or screenshots) | *supporting documents*, *records*, *data*. *Evidence* sounds like a court case. |
| `wasei-reform` | リフォーム | *We reformed the office.* | *We renovated the office.* (*reform* = 制度などの改革) |

## 3. Literal translations of set phrases

| slug | 日本語 | Literal (avoid) | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| `osewa` | お世話になっております | *Thank you for your continued support.* at the start of every email | Skip it with people you write to often. In a first email: *Thank you for your help with…* (be specific). |
| `yoroshiku` | よろしくお願いします | *Best regards and thank you in advance for your kind consideration.* | *Thanks,* / *Best regards,* / *Thank you for your help.* For a real request: *I'd appreciate your help with this.* |
| `kentou` | 検討します | *We will consider it.* (= polite no in Japanese) | If yes: *We'll review it and reply by Friday.* If no: say so kindly. English readers take *consider* literally and will wait. |
| `osoreirimasu` | 恐れ入りますが | *I'm afraid but…* | *Could you…?* / *I'm sorry, but…* |
| `oshiete` | 教えてください | *Please teach me…* | *Could you tell me…?* / *Could you let me know…?* (*teach* = 授業をする) |
| `kakunin` | ご確認ください | *Please confirm.* for "please take a look" | *Please review / take a look at…*; *confirm* = 正しいと確定させる |
| `otsukare` | お疲れ様です | *You must be tired.* | Usually skip. *Thanks for today.* / *Thanks for your work on this.* |
| `ganbaru` | 頑張ります | *I will do my best.* in every reply | Say the action: *I'll finish the draft by Monday.* |
| `ganbatte` | 頑張ってください | *Please do your best.* (sounds like an order to a weak performer) | *Good luck!* / *I'm sure it'll go well.* |
| `my-poor-english` | 拙い英語で恐縮ですが | *Sorry for my poor English.* | Delete. It makes the reader look for mistakes. |
| `i-think` | 〜と思います | *I think* in front of every statement | Keep it for real opinions. For facts and plans, state them: *The deadline is Friday.* |
| `please-be-careful` | お気をつけて | *Please be careful.* (sounds like a warning) | *Have a safe trip.* / *Take care.* |

## 4. Email and business conventions

- **Self-introduction** `self-intro`. *I am Tanaka of ABC Corporation* (ABC社の田中です) → *I'm Taro Tanaka from ABC Corporation*, or skip it if your signature says it and they know you.
- **Buried request** `request-last`. Japanese emails build context and end with the request. English readers want the request in the first two lines. Show the reordered version.
- **対策 = countermeasure** `countermeasure`. In English *countermeasure* sounds military or security-related. For quality problems use *corrective action*; for plans, *measures* or *steps*.
- **Over-apology** `over-apology`. *Sorry for the trouble*, *Sorry for bothering you*, *Sorry for the late reply* in one email. Keep one apology, only when something actually went wrong.
- **Indirect no**. *It may be a little difficult* (ちょっと難しい) is read as "possible with effort". If the answer is no, say *Unfortunately, we can't…* and give an alternative.
- **Name order and honorifics** `double-honorific`. *Mr. Tanaka-san* → *Mr. Tanaka* (one honorific). Family name with *Mr./Ms.*; given name only if they sign that way. Japanese writers can sign *Taro Yamada* or *YAMADA Taro*; stay consistent.
- **Subject lines**. *Question* or *Request* alone → say what about: *Question about the March invoice*.
- **Closings**. *Best regards,* covers almost every case. *Sincerely,* for formal letters. Avoid ending the body with *Thank you in advance* plus *Best regards* plus *Thank you* again.

## 5. Academic writing

- **Hedging overload** `over-hedge`. *It might be possible that this may suggest…* One hedge per claim.
- **Passive everywhere**. Japanese papers often avoid *we*. Most journals now accept *we* and active voice; it's shorter and clearer.
- **"In this paper" and "Firstly…"** openers in every paragraph; see `in-this-paper` in `chinese.md`, which applies equally.
- **Katakana technical terms** that differ from the English term. Many match (*simulation*, *algorithm*), but check the ones that don't: *ガイドライン* is fine, *マニュアル* is often *instructions* or *protocol* in a methods section, *デメリット* is *disadvantage* or *limitation*.
- **Reviewer responses**: Japanese authors often over-apologize to reviewers (*We are deeply sorry for our insufficient explanation*). Use *Thank you for this comment. We have clarified…* See `genres/academic.md`.

## 6. Mechanics

- Full-width characters: 。、「」（）：！？ and full-width spaces → half-width equivalents.
- Full-width digits (１２３) → 123.
- No space between a word and an opening parenthesis (*result(Table 1)*) → *result (Table 1)*.
- Japanese quotation marks 「」 → " ".
- The 〜 wave dash for ranges → an en dash: *10–20*, or *10 to 20*.
