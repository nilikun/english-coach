# Register lens

Register is how formal the text sounds. Non-native writers miss it in two directions: chat-style English in an email to a director, or 1950s office English (*Kindly be informed herewith*) in a message to a teammate. Both cost credibility.

Load this file when the text is a mismatch for its reader. For Singapore-specific usage also load `l1/singapore.md`; for Hong Kong and Taiwan, `l1/chinese-tw-hk.md`.

## Four levels

Decide which level the reader expects, then flag anything more than one level away.

| Level | Where | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Chat | Slack, Teams, WhatsApp with colleagues | *sure, will send by 5* |
| 2. Internal email | Teammates, your manager | *I'll send the updated file by 5 pm.* |
| 3. External email | Clients, professors, recruiters, other companies | *I will send you the updated file by 5 pm today.* |
| 4. Formal document | Reports, papers, proposals, official letters | *The updated file will be circulated by 17:00.* or *We will circulate…* |

Level 3 is the safe default for anyone the user doesn't know well. Contractions (*I'll*, *don't*) are normal at levels 1–3 and should be avoided in papers and formal reports.

## Too casual for the reader

| Casual | Problem | At level 3–4 |
|---|---|---|
| gonna, wanna, gotta, kinda | spoken only | *going to*, *want to*, *have to*, *somewhat* |
| u, ur, pls, thx, tmr, b4 | chat abbreviations | write the words in full |
| Hi guys / Hey all | casual, and *guys* isn't neutral for everyone | *Hi everyone*, *Hi all*, *Hi team* |
| ASAP | demanding upward or to clients | *by [specific time]* or *at your earliest convenience* |
| FYI (alone) | curt | *For your information,* + one line of why it matters |
| a lot of, lots of | informal in papers and reports | *many*, *much*, *a large number of*, or the number |
| stuff, things | vague | name the thing |
| get (get better, get a result) | vague in formal writing | *improve*, *obtain*, *receive* |
| really, so, super (as intensifiers) | spoken emphasis | delete it, or give the fact that makes it big |
| !!! or several ! | shouting | one at most, and only in warm, informal email |
| emoji | unprofessional outside chat | delete in email to clients, professors, seniors |
| No problem / No worries (to a senior) | casual | *You're welcome* or *Happy to help* |
| Let me know if any questions | missing words | *Let me know if you have any questions.* |

## Too stiff or old-fashioned

These come from English business letters of the 1960s and survive in South and Southeast Asian office templates. To most international readers they sound robotic or bossy.

| Stiff | Natural |
|---|---|
| Please be informed that… / Kindly be advised that… | Just state it. Or *Please note that…* |
| Please kindly… | *Please…* or *Could you…* (one politeness marker is enough) |
| Please find attached herewith… | *I've attached…* / *Please find X attached.* (fine; *herewith* is the stiff part) |
| The undersigned | *I* |
| Your prompt reply would be highly appreciated | *Could you reply by Friday?* |
| We wish to inform you that | delete it; start with the information |
| Revert (= reply) | *reply*, *get back to you*. In US/UK English *revert* means "go back to a previous state". |
| Do the needful | say exactly what you need done |
| Noted with thanks | *Thanks, noted.* or *Thank you, I'll do that.* |
| Dear Sir/Madam (when you know the name) | *Dear Ms. Lim* |
| Respected Sir | *Dear Professor Tan* / *Dear Mr. Tan* |

## Politeness ladder

Non-native writers often have one way to ask and use it everywhere. Offer the step that fits the reader.

| Function | Direct (level 1–2) | Polite (level 3) | Very formal (level 4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ask | Can you send the file? | Could you send me the file by Friday? | We would be grateful if you could provide the file by Friday. |
| Follow up | Any update on this? | I wanted to follow up on my email from Monday about X. | I am writing to follow up on our request of 3 March regarding X. |
| Say no | We can't do Friday. | Unfortunately, Friday won't work for us. Would Monday be possible? | Unfortunately, we are unable to meet the Friday deadline. We propose Monday, 10 March instead. |
| Disagree | I don't think that's right. | I see it a bit differently: … | We respectfully disagree with this assessment, because … |
| Apologize | Sorry for the delay. | Sorry for the late reply. | We apologize for the delay in responding. |
| Remind | Reminder: … | Just a reminder that X is due on Friday. | Please note that X is due on Friday, 14 March. |

Avoid stacking ladder steps (*Could you please kindly help to send…*). One polite marker per request.

## Spoken habits in writing

Speech habits carry into writing, and they read as careless. Flag them:

- Sentence starts with *So*, *Actually*, *Basically*, *Like*.
- *Very* + weak adjective chains (*very very important*).
- Tag questions (*…, right?*, *…, is it?*, *…, can?*) in an email.
- Run-on sentences joined by *and then … and then …*.

## What not to flag

- Contractions in an email (normal at levels 1–3).
- Starting a sentence with *And* or *But* in an email or report. It's fine. In a paper, use it sparingly.
- Local terms in a message that will only be read locally (Singapore *MC* to a Singapore HR team is fine). Flag them only if the reader may be overseas, and say why.
